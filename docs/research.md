## 0. Summary and the plan against the bar

### 0.1 The machine

**The Pornainen plant (Finland): 1 MW, 100 MWh, about 2,000 t of crushed soapstone in an insulated steel silo about 13 m high and 15 m wide**,
built by Polar Night Energy for the district-heating company Loviisan Lämpö, in operation since June 2025. Electricity heats air through
resistors, a closed air loop carries the heat down buried steel pipes into the stone, and later a fan pulls air back through the same pipes to an
air-to-water exchanger that feeds the village's heating network. **It stores heat, and gives back heat.** The equipment appears to sit **on a floor under the roof, directly above the stone** (§0.2, point 6).

Why this one and not the first silo of 2022 or the 250 MWh silo due in 2027 (§8, Q1): it is the only one with both a published bed
geometry (14 × 6 m, S6) and a first year of reported results (S5). The lab draws a **generic reconstruction of that class of machine**, not the
real plot: the maker publishes no pipe layout, no insulation thickness and no charging power.

### 0.2 Five things the other roles must know before they start

1. **The main video has no machine figure.** Its only numbers are 36 % of process heat and 100 Mt of CO₂e (K1, K2). So « the figures of the video
   are retrieved » is empty here; the tests retrieve the **maker's site and its lead scientist's article** instead (S2 to S6).
2. **The world's largest sand battery contains no sand.** Pornainen is crushed soapstone, a fireplace by-product. Real sand is the 2022 silo and the
   2027 one. The lab says so plainly and offers both media on the same silo (Q2).
3. **The roadmap's c ≈ 0.8 kJ/kg·K is the room-temperature value.** Measured sand goes from about 0.78 to 0.85 near room temperature up to 1.13 at
   500 °C and 1.19 at 600 °C; the mean over the real swing is **about 1.0** (K26). A constant 0.8 understates the energy by 20 %. The model uses `c(T)`.
4. **Same silo, 2.5 times the energy.** Pornainen and the Vääksy silo are almost the same size and mass (15 × 13 m, 2,000 t; 15 × 14 m, 2,400 t), yet
   store 100 and 250 MWh (K31): the implied swing is about 180 K against 350 K. And Pornainen's 100 MWh is only about 46 % of the heat in the stone
   above ambient at its reported 400 °C (K32). Why that is so (usable heat at rated power against heat content) is the best question the model can answer.
5. **Every efficiency figure comes from the maker or its customers.** I found no independent or peer-reviewed measurement of any real silo. The
   figures also disagree (K10, K22). The lab shows ranges and says who said what, once.
6. **The plant room is probably not a separate building at Pornainen.** A documentary films the engineer in the « equipment space » among resistor, blower and exchanger, saying the soapstone is « below us » (S31, ≈1:36, automatic transcription). A maker's photo shows two clad water pipes running down the silo's wall from high up to the ground (S14), its schematic draws the loop above the bed, the maker insists the ducts are kept short (S6), and this would explain why a 6 m bed sits in a 13 m structure (K6, K5). Vääksy, by contrast, gets a « separate technical building » (S10). **I recommend an equipment floor under the roof** (Q3); it remains a reading, marked as such.

### 0.5 A plant, not a kit: what that changes for the two guides

The visitor will never build this. Pornainen took about a year, about 40 subcontractors and over 100 workers (K18), and the maker says it has no product
for homes (S2). So the two modes stay (the frame is locked) but they change meaning:

- **Assembly** becomes **« how a crew builds it »**: the construction sequence of the plant, 20 steps, each saying what the step is **for** and what
  is checked. **Installation** becomes **« how it joins the town »**: 10 steps, from « does this site qualify » through the grid, the network, the slow first
  heating, the winter of tests, to the price algorithm.

How they stay honest:

1. **Said once, at the top of each guide**: this is the lab's reading of how such a plant goes up, not a plan to copy. Mill's assembly guide already does this.
2. **Every step carries its origin, in its text**: _reported_ (with its K number in the data, never in visitor text), _usual practice_ (generic civil and
   process engineering, in no maker document), or _our reconstruction_. `GuideStep` has no origin field and needs none: the last words of `text` carry it, and a
   data test can check that each step ends with one of the three.
3. **Only a few facts of the order are reported**: at Vääksy the foundations come before the silo and its technical building (S10); at Pornainen the filling ended in
   October 2024 with 40 truckloads, testing followed in winter, commissioning in June 2025 (S13, S4). **The rest of the sequence is ours**, and the guide says so.
4. **No recipe figures**: no weld, torque, cure time, steel grade, pipe count, insulation prescription or per-step duration. `torque` stays empty. `duration` is filled only for the
   reported totals. `tools` lists the trades and machines of the step (crane, concrete pump, tanker truck, commissioning engineer), which are generic and say so.
5. **BRIEF §8 applies**: mains and high temperature, so no « ready to build » dimension beyond what the source gives. Pipe pitch, insulation and sizes appear with
   their estimate mark, as outputs of a physics exercise, in the drawings and the sheets.
6. **The home question gets one honest answer**, in lesson 3 and a simulation preset, not a build guide: the same physics applied to two drums (K38).
7. **The mechanics still hold**: nothing floats at any step (pipes need their spacer before the stone arrives, the stone needs its shell), and Mill's test applies, every id of
   `silo`, `pipe-cell` and `plant-room` is installed in exactly one step.

### 0.6 Sheets, lessons, spoken guide

**Sheets (A3, from `dims.ts`, estimates marked):** 1 `general-arrangement` (plot, silo, annex, trench) · 2 `silo-section` (the layers, official and estimated dimensions told apart) ·
3 `temperature-maps` (the section at 0, 25, 50, 100 % charge, model output) · 4 `pipe-field` (plan, section, zones, expansion; all estimate) · 5 `air-loop` (P&ID in charge, discharge, both) ·
6 `single-line` (from the medium-voltage line to the resistors; 1,443 A at 400 V for 1 MW, K35) · 7 `heat-side` (exchanger, pump, meter, vessel, the pipes down the wall, boiler in parallel, down to a house) ·
8 `energy-balance` (Sankey of a cycle and a year, model output) · 9 `assembly-sequence` (exploded, numbered) · 10 `scale-comparison` (the four real silos and two drums to scale, A/V beside each).

**Lessons:** 1 `heat-in-stone` (E = m·c·ΔT, why c changes, the 100 and 250 MWh silos, stone against water: about 108 kWh/m³ against 58, K33) ·
2 `the-gradient` (slow conduction, the 1 m block in 8 days, charging the core first) · 3 `surface-and-volume` (RC, the half-life, the scale law, 60–75 % at 8 MWh against more than 85 %, the two drums) ·
4 `air-carries-the-power` (Q = ṁ·cₚ·ΔT, 1 MW needs about 3 kg/s, power against capacity, why not all the heat is usable) ·
5 `the-cheap-hours` (**the game**: paint your charging hours on a week of prices against the optimiser, then the solar overflow) ·
6 `heat-is-not-electricity` (30–35 % back to electricity, K40; an electric boiler, a heat pump and a water tank compared fairly, including when a sand battery is the wrong answer). Two quiz questions each.

**Spoken guide:** 51 parts × about 270 characters, a few star parts (`storage-bed`, `air-water-exchanger`, `resistor-bank`) at about 400, four worlds, an introduction: about 20,000 characters per language. Character to follow: **a single grain** that warms slowly and hands its heat to the next, in the Pelton manner. Anonymous as usual: « la vidéo », « les constructeurs » at most. Said once: reconstruction, generic pipe field, model figures. Written after the checkpoint, from the final sheets.

### 2.5 Conflicts between sources, and what the lab does

1. **Pornainen's temperature.** « 400 °C maximum storage temperature » (S3, undated page) · **average about 400 °C at full charge** (S6, 2025, the lead scientist's model) · core up to 500 °C (S22, 2024) · operates at 500 °C (S17) · sand up to 600 °C (S2, S18). ### 2.7 Sanity checks

- **Energy balance.** No plant gives out more than it takes in: all K10 and K22 values are at or below 1, and K11's 5 + 5 % gives 90 %, the figure the maker quotes for GWh scale, not for Pornainen. Fine, but note the 90 % is an upper limit of that sentence.
- **K7:** 2,000 t / 40 trucks = 50 t a truck, plausible for Finnish road limits (assumption).
- **K22:** 4 × 7 m is 88 m³, so 80 m³ fills it almost completely: **4 × 7 m is then the inner tank**, and the visible silo is larger. 100 t / 80 m³ = 1,250 kg/m³ is low for sand (S24's measured 1,425 gives 114 t): « about hundred tons » is a round figure.
- **K13:** 160 t = 70 % means about 229 t before and 69 t after. No check possible without the fuel split.
- **Half-life, K9, against pure conduction:** first diffusive mode of a 14 × 6 m cylinder with cold walls gives τ of about 80 to 100 days, half-life 55 to 72 days. With insulation it must be longer: three months is credible.
- **K24 and K23** agree (same mass, near-same size, the same 2 MW), so Vääksy is very probably that product. Not stated.
- **Nothing here measures a real silo independently** (§0.2 point 5). `compare` tests report, they do not validate.

## 5. Physics, for `lab-physicist`

Pure TypeScript, SI inside, cheap enough for a phone. Dimensions come from `dims.ts`. **Never tuned to the `compare` figures**: where the model disagrees, say so and why.

### 5.1 The bed

An axisymmetric (r, z) grid, about 10 × 6 cells, state = **enthalpy per cell**, so energy is conserved while c changes with T. Temperature from h(T) using K26 (sand) or K29 (soapstone). Conduction with k(T) from K28 and K29. At 0.7 m per cell and α about 3.7e-7 m²/s an explicit step is stable up to days, so one hour is comfortably inside. **There is no convection in a bed**: « stratification » here is a radial and axial gradient kept by slow conduction and by the charging order. It is unlike a water tank. Say so, for the chauffe-eau that follows.

The 3D reads the (r, z) array to colour the section: a small data texture, updated at a low rate. The `pipe-cell` world reads a finer analytical profile around one pipe.

### 5.2 Pipes to bed

The pipe field is subgrid. For each cell, conductance from the pipes in it: G = 2πkL / (ln(R/r) − 3/4) per pipe, with R the equivalent cell radius of the pitch. If bursts look too fast (charging in a few cheap hours), split each cell in a near-pipe and a far-pipe node. Air marches along z: T_out = T_cell + (T_in − T_cell)·exp(−G/(ṁ·cₚ)). Air properties from T.

### 5.3 The loops

- **Charge:** P_el → heater (η about 1, with `cableResistance` from the kit) → air up to about 500 °C (K44) → zones in order core, middle, outer.
- **Discharge:** zones in order outer, middle, core → exchanger → back through the blower.
- **Assumption, to state in `docs/physics.md`:** each zone can connect to either header, so charge and discharge run **at the same time on different zones**. The pilot swapped one loop with valves (S19), but a network with 100 % reliability (K13) cannot wait for a charge to end. If the maker contradicts this, one blower goes away.
- **Blower:** P = V̇·Δp/η, Δp ∝ ṁ², with the pressure drop from the pipes and ducts (estimate); its heat goes into the air.
- **Exchanger:** counterflow ε-NTU, sized for 1 MW at design temperatures. P_out = min(demand, ε·C_min·(T_air − T_ret)). **The usable capacity emerges from this**: when the bed can no longer hold both the rated power and the supply temperature. That is K4 against K32, and it is the honest answer to « why only 100 MWh ».
- **Losses:** each boundary cell loses through a series of layers, U from thickness and λ(T) plus the film, to air on the side and top, to ground below. Ducts lose only while flowing. Reported 5 % and 5 % (K11) are `compare`, not inputs.
- **Boiler:** picks up whatever the sand cannot deliver; this is what the scenarios count.

## 7. Safety, law and credits

**Hazards, for the sheets and one warning each, said once where the gesture is risky.**

- **Heat that does not show.** 400 to 600 °C inside, a bed that looks ordinary and stays hot for months. Hot air and duct surfaces. Nobody opens anything while it is hot.
- **Water and hot stone.** Moisture in the fill turns to steam on first heating; water on a hot bed is an explosion of steam. Hence dry fill, slow first heat.
- **Electricity.** 1,443 A at 400 V, medium voltage at the transformer, qualified staff only. A resistor bank with no air flow burns itself: the interlock of scenario 11.
- **Rotating blowers**, **pressurised hot water** of the network, **work at height** on the roof, **confined space** in the plenum and the shell.
- **Dust at filling.** Crystalline silica in fine sand and stone dust. EU law sets a limit on respirable crystalline silica (Directive (EU) 2017/2398, 0.1 mg/m³, from memory: **not verified in this research**, to re-read before quoting).
- **No combustion and no carbon monoxide at the silo itself**: the boiler kept as a backup keeps its own hazards.

**The home-build warning, one sentence and a lesson.** The maker has no home product (S2). Search results show video titles promising a two-drum store that heats a house all winter (not watched, not a source); the arithmetic of K38 says half a day. The lab teaches this and gives no build dimensions.

**Law, per country.** I found **no text specific to sand batteries**, and I am not qualified to classify one. The lab says « a plant like this is an industrial installation, subject to the rules of the country » and names nothing more unless a lawyer checks. What I can point at, to verify before any visitor sees it:

- **EU:** Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230, applying from 20 January 2027 and replacing Directive 2006/42/EC (S29, seen in a summary). Pressure Equipment Directive 2014/68/EU for a steam generator or a hot-water circuit, and Low Voltage Directive 2014/35/EU (from memory, **not verified**). ISO 13732-1 is the reference for burn thresholds on hot surfaces (scope read; the tables are paywalled, so no figure quoted).
- **Finland:** the real plant, Finnish grid and network rules; not looked up beyond Fingrid's market documents (S28).
- **France and Switzerland, the owner's visitors:** building permit for a 13 m structure, grid connection, classification of the installation: **not checked.**

**Credits and notice.** S1 and Polar Night Energy, with links, in the Sources panel; operators and papers as in §1. « Independent educational reconstruction; not an official drawing or a product of the maker; not affiliated. » « Sand Battery » with capitals is how the maker names its product, and the lab writes « batterie de sable » and « sand battery » in lower case as a generic term.
